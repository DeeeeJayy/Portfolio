02 · ABOUT

Who I Am

A cybersecurity practitioner who learns by doing — building tools, breaking systems, investigating problems, and documenting what matters.

Origin

I am Jagadeeswar Medidaraju — also known as DJ / Deeeejayy. I am a cybersecurity-focused individual with a strong interest in understanding technology, systems, and security from both practical and technical perspectives. I enjoy exploring how systems work, identifying weaknesses, investigating problems, and continuously developing my understanding through hands-on work.

What Got Me Here

My interest in cybersecurity grew from curiosity about how systems can be explored, tested, and secured. What initially attracted me was the investigative nature of the field — taking a system, understanding how it works, finding where assumptions can fail, and figuring out what is happening behind the scenes. Over time, that curiosity developed into a deeper interest in penetration testing, digital investigation, web security, reverse engineering, and security research.

Current Stage

I am currently developing myself as a cybersecurity professional while building practical knowledge and experience. My focus is on strengthening my technical foundation, gaining hands-on exposure, and working on meaningful security problems — gradually developing deeper expertise rather than specializing too narrowly too early.

17 · PERSONAL PHILOSOPHY

How I Think & Work

"Survive whatever means Necessary ( Learn || Copy || Steal )" — Understanding beats collecting certificates. Practical knowledge beats theoretical familiarity.
How I Learn
I prefer a hands-on and investigation-driven approach. Instead of only studying a concept theoretically, I try to understand how it behaves in a practical environment. I experiment, test different approaches, troubleshoot failures, and research the underlying concepts when something does not make sense.
Why I Build
Projects begin with a practical problem, a technical curiosity, or something I want to understand more deeply. Not every project becomes a product — some are practical utilities, some are learning exercises, some are experiments. Together they represent progression, not just output.
My Approach to Security
When approaching a security problem, I try to understand the system first rather than immediately reaching for a tool. The structured cycle is: Understand → Investigate → Experiment → Test → Learn → Improve. Tools serve methodology, not the other way around.
Continuous Improvement
Cybersecurity is a field where continuous learning is essential. Every unfamiliar system or problem is an opportunity to understand something new. Getting stuck is part of the process. Documenting what I learn — including failures — is how the knowledge compounds.
LEARNING CYCLE
Learn→ Practice→ Get Stuck→ Investigate→ Solve→ Document→ Improve
